What is PDF compression and why do you need it?

PDF compression reduces the file size of a PDF document by optimizing the images, fonts, and internal structure it contains — without changing how the document looks to the reader. A PDF full of high-resolution scans or photos can easily reach tens of megabytes, which makes it slow to email, hard to upload to size-limited forms, and heavy to store. Compressing it brings the size down dramatically while keeping the text sharp and readable.

Doctor PDF uses two strategies at once — it rebuilds the PDF structure and re-encodes the images — then automatically keeps whichever result is smaller. This means you get the best possible reduction without having to choose any technical settings yourself.

When do you need to compress a PDF?

  • Email attachments that exceed the provider's size limit (for example Gmail's 25 MB cap).
  • Government, university, or job-application forms that reject files over a few megabytes.
  • Uploading scanned contracts, IDs, or certificates that came out far too large.
  • Saving storage space on your phone or computer when archiving many documents.
  • Speeding up sharing over WhatsApp, Telegram, or slow internet connections.

How Doctor PDF compresses your files privately

Unlike most online compressors, Doctor PDF never uploads your file. The entire compression runs inside your browser using your own device's processor, so your documents — even sensitive ones like contracts or medical records — never leave your computer or reach any server.
